## Purpose of This Repository

This repository is an internal research record maintained by NMA Consulting Group to document the due diligence performed prior to and during the publication of the Crypto Trust Standard (CTS) framework.

Its purpose is to establish a clear, timestamped paper trail showing that:

1. NMA Consulting Group conducted extensive research across publicly available sources before publishing the CTS framework
2. No substantially identical bundled framework was found to exist prior to NMA's publication
3. NMA's specific contribution — the bundle of four scam-prevention layers into a single exchange-mandated standard — is an original work product
4. All prior art identified was explicitly acknowledged in the public-facing CTS documentation

This repository is **not** a legal opinion. It is a research record. If legal questions arise, this record should be reviewed with qualified intellectual property counsel.

---

## What Was Searched

Research covered the following categories:

- Published cryptocurrency safety and security standards
- Exchange listing requirement frameworks
- Token scam-prevention tools and platforms (liquidity locking, audit firms, anti-whale mechanisms, identity protocols)
- Regulatory and policy frameworks (SEC, IOSCO, MiCA, CFTC publications)
- Academic literature and white papers on crypto fraud prevention
- GitHub repositories for open-source crypto safety frameworks
- Industry consortium publications (CryptoConsortium, CCSS, etc.)
- Web search results across multiple query formulations targeting the specific CTS innovation

---

## Key Finding — Summary

**No framework was found that combines all four of the following into a single named, exchange-mandated standard:**

1. Liquidity locking with minimum duration and release rate requirements
2. Anti-whale enforcement with wallet caps and daily sell limits governed by supermajority vote
3. On-chain verification mandating live blockchain sourcing for all balance displays
4. Cryptographic endorsement registry with zk-proof KYC and wallet-signed promotional content

The individual components exist as separate, voluntary, uncoordinated tools. The bundle — and the exchange mandate behind it — does not exist elsewhere.
